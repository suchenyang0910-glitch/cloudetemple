The wooden fish in temple practice

The mõ is a hollow wooden percussion instrument struck to keep a steady rhythm during chanting. A common explanation of its fish shape is that fish never close their eyes, a reminder to stay awake and mindful. During chanting, the bell or bowl often marks the beginning, transitions and end, while the wooden fish keeps the pace. Strike lightly, about once per second, or with your breath.
